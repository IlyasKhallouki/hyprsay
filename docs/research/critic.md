# Completeness critic: Jev-driven Hyprland voice control research

Date: 2026-09-21. Author: critic subagent. Inputs: the nine `research_*.md` lane reports in this scratchpad, plus the main loop's live Jev output on disk (`jevlive/probe_out.json`, `jevlive/dayone_out.json`, `jevlive/followup_out.json` and the scripts next to them). I made no API calls and sent nothing to the desktop. Everything labeled LIVE below was read from those JSON files, not measured by me.

Trust reminder: jev-wire, jev-design, prior-art and nlu-arch had no verifier pass. stt, ui, hypruse-map, hypr-ipc and gateway-stt were verified, and every verified latency number turned out to be load dependent.

## 0. What the live data on disk already changes (nobody has folded this in yet)

These come from the main loop's own files and they overturn assumptions in several lanes.

1. The run to run noise is larger than the task brief says. `dayone_out.json` determinism block, 30 identical requests: P(w1) for the window target ranged 0.45 to 0.67 (16 distinct values), and the argmax flipped: w1 26 times, w2 4 times. The brief quotes 0.50 to 0.59, which was only the first 6 reps. Intent (p = 1.0) and the is_command boolean (0.90 to 0.92) were stable.
2. That noisy case is not an ambiguous utterance. It is "put the browser on workspace three" with exactly one Firefox window and the focused window being kitty (`probe.py`). The only live accuracy data point for window targeting therefore shows a should-be-easy semantic reference landing near a coin flip and picking the terminal 13 percent of the time. The probe's instruction had a competing clause ("Use focused_window when they do not name one"); `design.py` and `overlap.py` were written to explore fixes, but their output was not saved to disk, so the fix is not evidenced anywhere I can read.
3. Noise is per question, not per request. `followup_out.json` self_ensemble: 8 identical copies of one question inside a single request differ by up to 0.16 (median spread 0.08). Averaging 8 copies only cut the stdev from 0.059 to 0.040.
4. Latency grows with input tokens, contrary to the vendor line that size barely matters. LIVE p50 total: 315 ms at 725 tokens, 314 ms at 2.2k, 448 ms at 6.0k, 544 ms at 11.5k. Question count is nearly free (1 to 40 questions: 312 to 323 ms p50).
5. HTTP 503 correlates with request size. LIVE: 13 of 30 calls failed at 11.5k tokens, 1 of 30 at 6k, 0 at 2.2k and below; the interleaved follow up confirmed it (0 of 25 small, 4 of 25 large). The practical ceiling is far below the documented 32k/64k.
6. Tails exist at small sizes too: max 1,617 ms in a 30 call block at 837 tokens, plus the earlier 6.9 s outlier.
7. Three concurrent requests cost p50 359 ms wall versus about 315 ms for one.
8. The gateway reports provider time: p50 about 220 to 235 ms of the 315 ms is the gateway to TypeSafe leg. About 90 ms is this machine to the edge plus gateway overhead.
9. The gateway response never reveals the underlying model version. Both routes return `"model": "typesafe-ai/jev"`, while the direct API returns `jev-1.13.0`. Any design that keys caches or thresholds on `response.model` (nlu-arch section 12) cannot detect a silent alias move through the gateway.
10. The inferred confidence formula `(p_max - 1/n)/(1 - 1/n)` (jev-design 7.2, nlu-arch 6.4) holds for Choice on all 21 live Choice answers on disk, and fails on all 7 live Score answers (4 levels: p_max 0.76 gives 0.68 by formula, the service returned 0.75). So "we can compute confidence client side" is wrong for Score. Read the returned value; never recompute it.
11. Option order test (83 app options, 9 permutations): the choice never changed, top probability moved 0.68 to 0.83. Because identical requests alone move by about 0.2, this test cannot separate order bias from noise. Same for the independence check (alone mean 0.57, among 20 mean 0.53, n = 8 each): inconclusive.
12. `cost` is "0" on every live call because of the promotion that ends 2026-09-25; `marketCost` shows the list price. The account holds paid credits, so the free tier was never exercised.

## 1. Contradictions between lanes

C1. Streaming partials versus one-shot decode (the biggest one). nlu-arch (S1, S4, 13, 14) and gateway-stt (section 9) build the hot path on streaming ASR with stable partials, a stability signal, n-best and word timestamps, and get their best numbers from speculation on partials (345 ms and 0.25 s). The stt lane measured that streaming on this CPU is the wrong choice (moonshine-voice Tiny Streaming near 100 percent of real time, 190 to 715 ms finalize) and recommends buffering plus one offline decode with no partials at all. The ui lane's `hearing` state (partial transcript) and prior-art's "partial transcript within 100 ms" also assume partials. With the stt recommendation there is nothing to speculate on until the speculative decode at about 180 ms of silence, and grammar-aware adaptive endpointing ("the partial already parses") cannot exist.

C2. STT model default. stt report: Parakeet TDT 110M is the default. stt verifier: Parakeet slowed 3.2x under load (median 511 ms) and Moonshine Tiny is the more robust pick. gateway-stt: hot path is moonshine-voice Tiny Streaming. Three answers.

C3. N-best and hotword biasing. jev-design (`alternatives` in state) and stt (pass N-best into state) want alternatives. The stt lane's own finding is that the sherpa-onnx offline Moonshine path is greedy only, and hotwords are transducer only and inconclusive on Parakeet. If Moonshine Tiny wins C2, there is no n-best and no biasing.

C4. Latency budgets do not agree. nlu-arch: ASR finalization 40 ms [A], fast path 195 ms, PTT action 45 to 60 ms after release, Jev RTT 150 to 250 ms [A]. Measured: decode 120 to 166 ms (up to 372 to 845 ms under load), Jev 321 ms. stt: 0.5 s PTT and 0.65 s VAD. gateway-stt: 0.56 s, verifier 0.56 to 0.70 s. prior-art's 400 ms stretch goal is unreachable on the Jev path (decode plus Jev alone is about 440 to 490 ms before capture and dispatch). Only the local grammar path can meet it.

C5. Four lanes, four Jev deadlines. nlu-arch 450 ms after the final transcript (would drop roughly the slowest tenth of healthy calls at LIVE p90 356 to 439 ms), stt 1 s, jev-design 600 to 900 ms, jev-wire 1.5 s.

C6. One request versus state-sliced requests. jev-design, prior-art and the third-party projects: one request per utterance, at most one in flight. nlu-arch: R1, R2, R3 concurrently, plus per-clause sets, plus hedges, up to 6 streams and 4 speculative sets. LIVE: concurrency costs about 45 ms and multiplies 503 exposure; large requests are what fail.

C7. App candidates. jev-design and prior-art: fuzzy or phonetic prefilter to the top 10 to 15 apps. nlu-arch: all 86 visible `.desktop` entries in one Choice, plus a flat joint action Choice of about 180 options. A lexical prefilter removes exactly the cases Jev is there for ("the browser", "file manager", a badly misheard name). Neither side has accuracy data.

C8. Default route. jev-wire: route B (`/typesafe/v1/systemone`, noul) as default so the direct API is a config switch. nlu-arch: route A (`/v1/evaluate`), matching Vercel's advice for new code. LIVE: A p50 335 ms, B 316 ms, n = 30 each, within noise; the one failed full probe was on A.

C9. Keepalive policy. nlu-arch: ping every 20 to 30 s while armed. jev-wire: warm on push to talk press, detect GOAWAY, `keepalive_expiry=120`. The idle timeout is unknown, so neither is grounded.

C10. Memoization. nlu-arch section 12: "local memoization gives determinism on our side". With LIVE per-call noise of about 0.2 on a near tie, caching one sample freezes a coin flip, possibly the wrong side, until the state signature changes.

C11. Dead band size. nlu-arch uses a 0.05 dead band and a 0.15 margin gate, justified by vendor wobble of 0.01 to 0.05. LIVE wobble spans 0.22, so the top-two margin swings by about 0.4 and those gates will flicker between act, hints and no-op on the same utterance. jev-design's claim that a 0.60 abstain floor yields 99.2 percent decision agreement is a vendor figure that the LIVE case (0.45 to 0.67 straddles 0.60) does not support.

C12. Destructive actions. jev-design: R2 acts automatically at 0.9 or above. nlu-arch: never on Jev alone, lexical anchor plus a 1.5 s cancellable countdown. prior-art: always hold with a standalone spoken "confirm", and session or system actions are never offered to the model at all. nlu-arch and jev-design both put close, exit and power into the intent Choice. Tier membership also differs (launch is tier 0 in prior-art, tier 1 in nlu-arch; typing is R2 in jev-design, tier 1 in nlu-arch).

C13. Ambiguity policy. prior-art P0 5: for reversible actions act on the top guess and let a spoken number swap it (Serenade). nlu-arch and jev-design: do not act, show numbered hints. Note prior-art's own finding that no window-action undo exists yet, which the Serenade variant depends on.

C14. HUD contract. ui: bottom center, one-way display-only protocol, permanently empty input region, engine never waits on it. prior-art: top center, click-through "except while a confirm card is up", and a click on the HUD as the non-voice confirm path. A display-only HUD cannot take that click.

C15. Push to talk transport. stt and prior-art: `bind`/`bindr` that `exec` a CLI verb (`voicectl ptt-up`). hypruse-map measured a cold Python CLI start at 95 to 240 ms, which lands directly on the endpoint at key release. hypr-ipc section 11 gives the zero-spawn path (`event` dispatcher plus socket2 `custom>>`). Nobody connected the two. Also, the stt verifier notes no release bind is configured here, so `bindr` was never exercised live.

C16. Echo cancellation. stt: no AEC by default, duck the sink during push to talk. nlu-arch section 9 lists AEC as part of prompt-injection hardening; prior-art wants it for hands-free. In open mic mode the default-off stance leaves speaker audio as a command channel.

C17. Lua provider. hypr-ipc: two renderers are mandatory, 0.56.2 source says `.conf` goes away in 0.57. hypruse-map lists "should the fork keep Lua support?" as open. ui and hypr-ipc both state that no Lua string was ever executed (this machine is hyprlang). nlu-arch's plan fusion (`exec [workspace 2 silent] firefox`) is flagged unconfirmed for Lua. So the mandatory path is 100 percent unexecuted.

C18. Interpreter and packaging. ui: the HUD must run on the system interpreter (system PyGObject). stt: "sherpa-onnx on system Python 3.14", but it benchmarked from a venv, and Arch's system Python is externally managed (pip installs need a venv). hypruse-map: rewrite a11y over the Atspi GI typelib "on the system interpreter". The engine therefore needs pip-only wheels (sherpa-onnx, sounddevice, h2) and, if the a11y rewrite happens, system-only `gi`. No lane resolves this (venv with system site packages, a third helper process, or staying on busctl).

C19. Snapshot contents. hypruse-map: hypruse's snapshot lacks `focusHistoryID`, workspace names, `initialClass`. hypr-ipc: the compositor provides them. Not a factual conflict, but jev-design's `previous_window` and nlu-arch's focus-history rank depend on a field the inherited `snapshot_from` drops. It must be added.

C20. Minor numeric or enumeration disagreements that do not change conclusions: hyprctl spawn cost 6 ms (hypr-ipc) versus 15 to 22 ms (verifier, nlu-arch, hypruse-map); hypruse-map's "every postEvent name" list is incomplete (it omits createworkspace, destroyworkspace, changefloatingmode, bell, kill, renameworkspace, togglegroup, changeworkspaceid that ui and hypr-ipc found; hypr-ipc counts 45 with v2 variants). "No lock event on socket2" holds in all three. hypruse-map's open question on when `hyprctl locked` shipped is answered by hypr-ipc (commit 064bdb06f, 2024-05-13), though the verifier did not recheck that date.

## 2. Load-bearing claims still unverified

Settled and therefore NOT listed: routes A and B work with a gateway key, both return TypeSafe confidence, warm median about 320 ms, question count is nearly free, answers are not byte identical.

U1. Jev window-target accuracy. Every lane assumes "Jev absorbs paraphrase and ASR errors" (stt section 6 says "focus kiddy" is "still resolved correctly"). Zero evidence; the single LIVE data point points the other way (section 0 item 2). No labeled corpus exists.
U2. Jev on real ASR output (homophones, split words, no punctuation, Moonshine's "Workspace 3." casing and digits). Not found in any source, never tested.
U3. Threshold values. Every act, confirm, reject number in nlu-arch, jev-design and prior-art comes from vendor cookbooks (some on jev-1.12) or third-party READMEs tuned for web elements. None is calibrated for this domain, and the LIVE noise invalidates the margin arithmetic.
U4. Model pinning through the gateway (`jev-1.13.0`, `typesafe-ai/jev-latest`, `jev`). Unknown, and the gateway response hides the version (section 0 item 9), so drift is also undetectable.
U5. Prompt injection resistance. One vendor sentence ("does not treat state as hostile by default") and one third-party anecdote. No test with adversarial titles, in state or in criteria.
U6. The 503 mechanism: is it size, rate, or the promotional capacity pool? Does an immediate retry succeed? Is it the same on route B? Only one account, one morning.
U7. Tail latency at production size over hours and times of day (p95, p99), and whether hedging helps or just doubles load on a service that is already returning 503.
U8. HTTP/2 idle timeout at the Vercel edge, behavior after suspend and resume, and cost of the first call after each.
U9. Billing after 2026-09-25: error shape at zero balance, free-tier eligibility for other users of a published app, rate limits under speculation, and whether aborted requests are billed.
U10. Direct TypeSafe path (latency from Europe, pinning, fallback). No direct key exists. LIVE provider time of about 220 ms suggests direct would land near 280 to 300 ms, which is inference.
U11. STT accuracy. 8 clean synthetic Kokoro TTS commands, on a machine at loadavg 6 to 27. No real microphone, no fan or key noise, no owner's accent, no rare app names. "8 of 8" needed normalization to be true. No benchmark ran on an idle machine.
U12. Human pause tolerance for the 350 ms commit and 180 ms speculative trigger. A design estimate.
U13. No dispatch was ever sent over the raw socket by any lane (all read-only). The wire spelling is from source only. `ok` for stale addresses, the 1023-byte multiple hang, and the 5 s main-thread poll stall are all inferred from code.
U14. Every Lua-provider string (dispatch expressions, `hl.layer_rule`, `hl.dsp.window.set_prop`, exec rules). Read from source and stubs, never executed. Several field names (dpms, group lock, pin, cycle prev) were not even read.
U15. Animation suppression per action (`keyword animations:enabled 0` in a batch). Untested, and impossible under Lua as written. The 500 to 600 ms tween is the largest visible cost.
U16. All acting latencies (dispatch round trip, click, wtype spawn, launch to window), and every a11y timing. The AT-SPI registry on this machine has been broken since 2026-09-13, so `ui`, `marks`, `click_ui`, nlu-arch's R3 request and the strict password-field guard cannot be demoed or measured at all.
U17. Whether individual dispatchers gate on the session lock (only the generic IPC path was audited), and the `locked` gate under Omarchy 4's Quickshell locker.
U18. HUD: fractional scale and multi-monitor geometry, a real button press through the surface, blur cost on the UHD 620, direct scanout loss under fullscreen video, SIGTERM under a busy GTK loop, the `create_asyncio_task` hang on 3.14.7.
U19. Gateway STT: team beta access, raw key in the WebSocket subprotocol, audio-done to finish latency for all three streaming models, whether the socket can stay warm. Only relevant if the cloud second-opinion path survives.
U20. Literature and secondary items that prior-art could not open (Talon leaving Linux, numen grammar, Funk et al.) and the citations nlu-arch recalled from memory without refetching.

## 3. Risks nobody covered (or covered in one sentence)

R1. Privacy of desktop state sent to a cloud model. Every Jev request carries window titles: mail subjects, page titles, chat contact names, document and repo names, private-browsing titles, password manager entry names. nlu-arch's R3 adds a11y control labels and, since hypruse `ui` reports current values, possibly field contents. Requests go from an EU machine (Paris edge) through Vercel to TypeSafe in AWS us-west-2. The catalog says `zdr: all` and `no_training: all` and a `zeroDataRetention` provider option exists, but no lane analyzed what Vercel's gateway observability stores, whether the option must be sent per request, whether it works on route B, or TypeSafe's own retention terms. Speculative calls, hedges and keepalives multiply the exposure. Missing design: per-class redaction (password managers, private windows, banking), a user denylist, titles only when needed to break a tie, a "what was sent" inspector, and a rule for the local logs (nlu-arch cassettes, negative cache, prior-art's JSONL history all persist transcripts and titles; only hypruse's journal hashes typed text).
R2. Third-party speech. In open mic mode other people's speech is transcribed and, if it passes the local gate, sent to a cloud service without their consent. gateway-stt's fallback would send raw audio. prior-art states the principle (only gated transcripts leave the machine); nobody specified the gate.
R3. Offline and degraded operation. One sentence in nlu-arch. Not designed: fast offline detection, captive portals, DNS failure at key press, the dead HTTP/2 connection after resume, a 43 percent 503 rate on big requests, zero credits after the promotion, and single-vendor risk on a 6 day old model behind an `experimental_` API whose alias can move silently. jev-wire mentions TypeSafe's LLM-backed adapter as a possible fallback evaluator; nobody evaluated it or a local classifier.
R4. Microphone handling. On native Arch with PipeWire there is no permission prompt or portal, so the app is the only honest indicator. stt recommends keeping the capture stream open permanently for pre-roll, even with push to talk, which undercuts prior-art's "privacy is obvious with PTT", keeps mic indicators lit, keeps the device from suspending, and on a Bluetooth headset forces the HSP/HFP profile and wrecks playback quality for as long as the daemon runs. Also unaddressed: hardware mic mute (silent failure, needs a HUD state), default source changes and hot-plug, and the hard mic mute that hypruse-map says the fork needs but nobody designed.
R5. Voice as an unauthenticated input channel. No speaker verification was considered. Anyone in the room, a video, a voice message or an autoplaying web page can speak commands in open mic mode, and the same audio can also say "confirm". Command classes that reach arbitrary code execution were not risk-ranked: `launch` takes a shell string, `type_text` into a focused terminal plus Enter, and `use_bind` runs any of the user's 131 binds, including ones bound to `exec` scripts, power menus or `exit`. nlu-arch offers all binds to Jev as options; the hypr-ipc destructiveness table covers dispatchers, not the user's binds. Under the Lua provider, speech-derived strings are evaluated inside the compositor (hypruse-map section 9 notes the existing blacklist for one argument only).
R6. Prompt injection beyond intent steering. nlu-arch's lexical anchor protects the intent tier only. A hostile title can steer the TARGET: make the attacker's window the one that gets focused, closed, or typed into, which combined with dictation is an exfiltration path. The live probe puts `class: title` into the option descriptions, the place with the most leverage. a11y labels, values and notification text are equally untrusted. No lane proposed structural defenses (resolve by class first, include titles only to break ties among same-class windows, drop titles for typing and destructive intents) and nobody tested any.
R7. API key handling. No lane says where the gateway key lives (systemd unit environment, plaintext config, libsecret), how it stays out of the journal, cassettes and bug reports, or what a leaked key can spend. The scratchpad scripts read it from the environment.
R8. Auth prompts. hypruse's auth interlock is blind to Quickshell polkit agents (Omarchy 4) per project memory, and the strict password-field guard depends on the a11y bus that is broken on this machine. Dictation into an auth prompt, and dictated secrets in logs, are open.
R9. Licensing. The fork itself is fine: hypruse is MIT and the owner holds the copyright. Not consolidated anywhere: Parakeet 110M is CC-BY-4.0 (attribution duty if bundled or auto-downloaded; the verifier did not recheck the card), Moonshine English is MIT (other languages are not), Kroko is CC-BY-SA and unverified, TEN VAD is not OSI clean, openWakeWord models are non-commercial, PyGObject and GTK and Qt are LGPL. Also open: model distribution (bundle versus first-run download, pinned checksums, supply chain), earcon asset licenses, and whether TypeSafe's and Vercel's terms allow a redistributed bring-your-own-key desktop controller. Onboarding cost for other users (Vercel account, credits after 2026-09-25) was not considered.
R10. Naming. The ui lane hardcoded `hyprvoice` (layer namespaces, service unit, app id). prior-art lists `hyprvoice` as an existing Hyprland dictation project (273 stars, archived 2026). No lane checked GitHub, AUR or PyPI for the final name, and nearby names are crowded (hyprwhspr, voxtype, omarchy-voice, omause, oc-voice, jev-voice, jev-voice-control). Putting "Jev" or "TypeSafe" in the name would be a third-party mark. Renames already identified by hypruse-map (runtime dir, PyPI workflow, `pkill -f hypruse`) should be extended to the `HYPRUSE_*` env prefix.
R11. Power and thermals on a 15 W laptop. Always-open capture, continuous VAD in hands-free mode, keepalive pings, a socket2 reader, 10 to 20 Hz polling in hint mode and a resident GTK process. Nobody measured idle draw of the recommended pipeline.
R12. Non-native English. Jev is English first and Moonshine Tiny is English only with a 12.66 percent vendor average WER on native benchmarks. The owner's accent was never tested; stt lists it as open but no lane treats it as a go or no-go risk.
R13. Test infrastructure. No labeled utterance corpus, no ambient false-accept corpus, and Hyprland integration tests need a QEMU virtio-gpu VM (project memory). The 652 hermetic hypruse tests do not cover any of the new surfaces.
R14. Coexistence. Four hypruse MCP servers run on this machine and share runtime files and global state (covered by hypruse-map), but no lane considered a voice daemon and an MCP agent acting on the same desktop at the same moment (two actors, one cursor, one focus).

## 4. The three decisions where the evidence is weakest

D1. The speech to text architecture and model (offline one-shot Moonshine Tiny or Parakeet 110M, versus streaming with partials). Evidence: 8 synthetic TTS clips on a loaded machine, no real microphone, no accent, no noise, and three lanes that disagree (C1, C2, C3). This choice also decides whether speculation on partials, adaptive endpointing, n-best and the HUD's live transcript can exist at all. Cheapest fix: record 100 to 200 real commands from the owner's laptop mic and run both offline models plus streaming Tiny on an idle machine.

D2. The Jev question design and gating numbers (one request versus sliced, prefilter versus full option lists, act or hint thresholds, margins, dead bands, memoization). Evidence: vendor cookbooks and week-old READMEs from other domains. The only live accuracy point is bad (0.45 to 0.67 on an easy target, 4 wrong picks in 30), live noise is four times the assumed dead band, large requests fail with 503, and the model version can be neither pinned nor observed through the gateway. Nothing should be frozen before a labeled replay set exists and the `design.py` comparison is rerun with its output saved.

D3. Activation, endpointing and the latency budget (hold to talk default, exec-based `bindr`, 350 ms commit with a 180 ms speculative trigger, 0.5 s and 0.65 s totals, deadlines from 450 ms to 1.5 s). Evidence: design estimates only. `bindr` was never exercised, the exec CLI path silently adds a Python cold start to the endpoint (C15), human pause tolerance is unmeasured, idle-machine numbers do not exist, and the 500 to 600 ms compositor animation that dominates what the user sees has no tested mitigation.

Runner up: gateway versus direct TypeSafe as the primary route (no direct key, so zero comparative data), and how much Lua-provider work to do now (mandatory per source text, fully unexecuted).

## 5. What can only be settled with the live key (for the main loop)

K1. Rerun `design.py` and `overlap.py` and save the output; then window-target accuracy on a labeled set of at least 100 utterances with semantic references, deictics and same-class windows, comparing question designs.
K2. Adversarial titles: intent steering and target steering, titles in state versus in criteria, with and without the "reference data" note.
K3. Model ids: `typesafe-ai/jev-1.13.0`, `jev-1.13.0`, `typesafe-ai/jev-latest`, `jev`; and check response headers for any version string.
K4. 503 versus tokens between 2k and 12k in 1k steps, on both routes, with an immediate single retry; record retry success.
K5. Tail latency: several hundred calls at the real request size spread over a day; p95 and p99; hedged versus not.
K6. Idle timeout: one call after 30 s, 60 s, 2, 5, 15 minutes idle on the same connection (`verify-jev-wire/idle_probe.py` exists), and after suspend and resume.
K7. Noise characterization near thresholds: how many identical copies are needed before the decision (not the probability) is stable, and the token cost of that.
K8. Order bias and independence with enough repeats per condition (at least 30) to beat the 0.2 noise.
K9. Real ASR transcripts (from the recorded corpus) as state, with and without alternatives.
K10. `providerOptions.gateway.zeroDataRetention` on both routes: accepted, and any latency or availability effect.
K11. After 2026-09-25: confirm cost becomes nonzero, and capture the error shape at zero balance on a throwaway key.
K12. Abort billing: cancel 50 in-flight requests and compare reported usage.
K13. Only if the cloud STT fallback stays in scope: beta access, raw key over the WebSocket subprotocol, audio-done to finish latency for the three streaming models.
K14. Needs a second credential (TypeSafe direct key): latency from this machine, pinning to `jev-1.13.0`, and use as a fallback path.
